For the past year or so I've bought many of my grocery staples at ALDI. I love the efficiency of the place -- one might even say it approaches elegance in its simplicity, purity, and predictability. Not unlike the programming language Haskell, which I'm reminded of every time I visit. Here's the ALDI logo:

<img src="/images/aldi-logo.png" alt="ALDI logo" width="200" />

And the Haskell logo:

<img src="/images/haskell-logo.png" alt="Haskell logo" width="200" />

The Haskell logo combines the monadic bind operator `>>=` with the lambda `λ` from lambda calculus. ALDI of course is just a stylized section of the letter `A`. In addition to sharing some of the same angles, the "ribboned" or "layered" aesthetic jumps out immediately. And though the color palettes are distinct, they share a boldness that's often absent in our drably colored modern era.

The Haskell logo was adopted [in early 2009](https://wiki.haskell.org/Haskell_logos/New_logo_ideas) following a community-driven competition to modernize the language's branding. The ALDI logo above [rolled out in 2017](https://www.supermarketnews.com/new-stores/aldi-modernizes-logo-to-match-store-changes):

>the company described \[the new logo\] as a “contemporary and future-oriented” interpretation of its current logo

I guess the future is indeed functional, if even grocers can see it...
